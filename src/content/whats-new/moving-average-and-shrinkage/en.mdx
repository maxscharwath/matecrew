Two things were wrong with the price. Both are fixed.

## The price no longer drags the past around

Until now an item was billed at the average of **every** purchase ever made. A
bulk order from February kept pushing the price up months after its last can was
gone.

<Compare
  beforeLabel="Before"
  afterLabel="Now"
  before="Average of every purchase ever. The price crept towards the real one very slowly, sometimes never."
  after="Moving average over the stock still in the fridge. An order leaves the calculation as soon as its cans are drunk."
/>

On a real case: three orders at 1.5336, 1.5334 and then 1.42, the old model
billed 1.52 for cans that had cost 1.42. The new one bills 1.48, and drops to
1.42 as soon as the leftovers from the older order run out.

<TrendChart
  title="Billed price per can across orders"
  unit=" CHF"
  caption="The price follows the actual stock instead of remembering cans drunk long ago."
  data={[
    { label: "5 Feb", value: 1.53 },
    { label: "28 Apr", value: 1.53 },
    { label: "7 Jul", value: 1.48 },
    { label: "after", value: 1.42 },

/>

Unconsumed stock keeps its value and carries it into the next period, where it
will actually be drunk. Drinking a can does not move the average — only a
delivery does.

## The price evolution applies to every item

An item that had never been bought was billed at **CHF 0.00**: nobody paid, and
the buyer swallowed the difference. An item with no history of its own is now
billed at the office-wide price at that moment.

On the Purchases chart, every item now has a line across the whole timeline.
Dashed where the price comes from the office-wide average, solid once the item
has purchases of its own, with a dot at every order that contained it.

## The cans that vanish

This was the real hole: MateCrew only billed a can if somebody recorded it. A can
taken without being declared was billed to nobody, and whoever fronted the money
for the order never saw it again.

<Callout>
  A new **Inventory** screen lets you count the fridge. The gap between the count
  and what the app believes is shared out across the people who drank during the
  period, in proportion to how much they drank. The buyer is reimbursed in full.
</Callout>

The existing stock adjustment stays what it was: a free bookkeeping repair with
no effect on money. Billing is what the inventory count does.

Every count is kept, with its gap in cans and in francs, valued at the price the
cans were carrying that day. The gap also shows on the reimbursement period it
falls in, and everyone sees their share of the shrinkage next to their share of
what they drank.
